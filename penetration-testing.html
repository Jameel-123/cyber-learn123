Penetration Testing

Penetration testing is the authorised process of assessing systems, applications, or networks to identify security weaknesses before attackers can exploit them.

Ethical hackers and penetration testers follow a structured process to safely analyse environments and report findings professionally.

What is Penetration Testing?

A penetration test simulates realistic attack techniques in a controlled, authorised, and legal manner.

The goal is to:

  • Identify vulnerabilities
  • Understand security weaknesses
  • Measure risk exposure
  • Recommend improvements
Penetration testing should only be performed on systems where permission has been granted.

1. Planning and Scope

Before testing begins, the tester must define the scope. The scope explains what systems are authorised for testing.

The planning stage may include:

  • Target systems
  • Testing objectives
  • Timeframes
  • Rules of engagement
  • Permissions and agreements

2. Reconnaissance

Reconnaissance involves gathering information about the target.

This may include:

  • IP addresses
  • Domain names
  • Public records
  • Exposed services
  • Technology stacks
Example: whois example.com

3. Scanning

Scanning identifies active systems, open ports, and running services.

Example: nmap -sV 192.168.1.10

The tester analyses the exposed services to understand possible risks.

4. Enumeration

Enumeration collects detailed information from services.

Examples include:

  • User accounts
  • Shared resources
  • Operating system information
  • Application versions

5. Vulnerability Analysis

After gathering information, the tester identifies vulnerabilities and misconfigurations.

Common weaknesses include:

  • Weak passwords
  • Outdated software
  • Exposed services
  • Poor configurations

6. Reporting

Reporting is one of the most important stages. Findings should be explained clearly and professionally.

Reports usually include:

  • Risk level
  • Evidence
  • Technical explanation
  • Impact assessment
  • Remediation advice

7. Remediation

Remediation means fixing identified weaknesses to reduce security risk.

Examples include:

  • Applying patches
  • Removing vulnerable services
  • Improving passwords
  • Changing configurations

Ethics and Legal Considerations

Ethical hacking requires clear authorisation. Unauthorised testing may be illegal and unethical.

Professional testers must:

  • Remain within scope
  • Protect collected information
  • Document findings responsibly
  • Act professionally at all times
