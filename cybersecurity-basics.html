Cybersecurity Basics

Cybersecurity is the process of protecting computers, networks, applications, and data from unauthorised access, misuse, attack, or damage.

Modern organisations rely heavily on digital systems, meaning security has become extremely important for businesses, governments, and individual users.

What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity focuses on protecting information systems and ensuring systems remain secure, available, and trustworthy.

Security professionals work to identify risks, reduce vulnerabilities, and respond to threats before they can cause harm.

Cybersecurity includes many different areas such as:

  • Network Security
  • Web Security
  • Application Security
  • Cloud Security
  • Digital Forensics
  • Penetration Testing
  • Incident Response

The CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is one of the most important security concepts. It explains three core goals of cybersecurity.

1. Confidentiality

Confidentiality means keeping information private and ensuring only authorised users can access sensitive data.

Example: Password-protected accounts and encrypted files

2. Integrity

Integrity means ensuring information remains accurate and is not modified without permission.

Example: Detecting unauthorised file changes

3. Availability

Availability means systems and services remain accessible when users need them.

Example: Keeping websites online during high traffic

Common Cyber Threats

Malware

Malware is malicious software designed to damage systems, steal information, or disrupt services.

  • Viruses
  • Worms
  • Ransomware
  • Trojans
  • Spyware

Phishing

Phishing attacks trick users into revealing sensitive information such as passwords or banking details.

Social Engineering

Social engineering attacks manipulate people rather than systems. Attackers may impersonate trusted individuals to gain access.

Password Attacks

Weak passwords can be guessed or cracked using automated tools.

Password Security

Strong passwords are one of the most basic but important security controls.

Good Password Practices

  • Use long passwords
  • Use upper and lowercase letters
  • Include numbers and symbols
  • Avoid common words
  • Do not reuse passwords
Example Strong Password: Cyb3r!Learn2026#

Ethical Hacking

Ethical hacking means testing systems legally and with permission to identify weaknesses before attackers can abuse them.

Ethical hackers follow rules, remain within scope, and provide reports explaining findings and recommendations.

Ethical hacking should only be performed on systems you own or have explicit permission to test.
